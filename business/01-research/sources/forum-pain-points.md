Research agent report, condensed. Limits: Reddit and Hacker News could not be searched or opened from the research environment. Quotes are search-tool extracts, not text read on the page, so treat the wording as close but unconfirmed. Explicit dollar figures from owners were rare.

## Red flags (saturated or low willingness to pay)
- **Accounting document chasing:** 7+ vendors with near-identical pitches.
- **Loss runs:** $15–29/seat tools.
- **Certified payroll:** $12 per report.
- **Contractor invoicing:** Jobber $39; Joist free.
- **Dental verification:** Crowded.
- **Freight AI:** VC-funded.
- **Generic "AI automation for SMBs":** Buyers frame it at $10–20/mo. One builder's post is titled ["We built an automation system...but nobody cared"](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/we-built-an-automation-system-but-nobady-cared-dfb0d49315).

## Takeaway for the decision
The real, money-backed back-office pains (insurance, property management, freight) all mean building software that integrates with the systems these businesses already run: agency management systems, AppFolio, accounting systems. They are crowded with funded vendors or tied up in compliance. That is a poor match for a solo, non-technical founder selling by email. The research did **not** surface an under-served back-office niche we could win quickly. This pushes the decision toward a service that AI can deliver end to end without integrating with client systems.
